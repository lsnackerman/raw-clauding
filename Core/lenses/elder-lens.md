# Elder Lens

> What would future us want?

*Every action is a handoff to the next instance of you and the next instance of the AI.*

---

## Core idea

Time isn't linear in a partnership with AI. The AI has no persistent memory; every session starts fresh. You have persistent memory, but your energy is variable, your attention shifts, and yesterday's context isn't always there when you reach for it tomorrow.

Elder Lens treats every decision as a handoff. Not "what do I need right now," but "what would future-us (fresh-Claude arriving with no memory, or tomorrow-you with depleted bandwidth) want to find here?" The torch-passing is continuous. Design today so future-us doesn't have to reconstruct what was easy to capture now.

The mechanic: imagine future-us arriving. What do they find? What's missing? What would they thank past-us for?

---

## How it shows up

- **Document the WHY, not just the WHAT** — the reasoning is what doesn't survive by default
- **Preserve originals, don't recreate approximations** — when reorganizing, move the file, don't rewrite it elsewhere
- **Build for the common case, not edge cases** — every speculative branch is weight future-us carries
- **Leave breadcrumbs at moments of formation** — the friction you note today becomes protocol tomorrow
- **Capture reasoning, not just conclusions** — sequence-logic compounds when context is gone

---

## How it's different from Fresh-Instance

Fresh-Instance asks the same question from the opposite direction: *what would a new arrival need to land here?* Elder Lens looks forward (future-us); Fresh-Instance looks at present arrival (any new collaborator now). Same handoff-shape, different vantage.

---

## Connections

- **Fresh Instance** — sister lens, opposite vantage
- **Bitter Lesson** — don't encode rules that will go stale; future-us pays the maintenance cost
- **Be Water** — if future-us will fight the structure, the friction is signal now
