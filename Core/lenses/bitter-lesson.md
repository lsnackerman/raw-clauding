# Bitter Lesson

> Are we encoding patterns, or rules that will go stale?

*General methods scale. Hand-crafted rules don't.*

---

## Core idea

Richard Sutton's "The Bitter Lesson" (2019) named a pattern across 70 years of AI research: every time researchers tried to encode human knowledge directly into systems (chess strategies, language rules, vision heuristics) they were eventually beaten by simpler approaches that just used more compute and let patterns emerge from data.

Applied to thinking partnerships: don't try to encode *how you think* into prescriptive rules and decision trees. Let the AI find patterns through search and conversation. Encode the *substrate* (folders, files, conventions) and let the *activity* emerge.

The bitter part: it feels productive to write detailed instructions, decision trees, "if this then that" rules. Those things go stale fast. The structure that survives is the structure that doesn't try to think for you.

---

## How it shows up

- **Patterns over rules** — a folder convention you can grep beats a 50-line instruction file
- **Meta-methods over hardcoded steps** — "search existing structure" beats "the answer is in `/path/to/specific/file.md`"
- **Demonstrate, don't prescribe** — if you have to write "be concise," the system isn't modeling conciseness
- **Trust capability** — don't encode what the AI already knows how to do
- **The thinner version usually wins** — when in doubt, less prescription

---

## Anti-patterns

- 500-line decision trees mirroring your mental model
- Rigid templates that crack the moment context shifts
- Rules that try to anticipate every edge case
- Workflows that assume one right path

## Connections

- **North Star** — Bitter Lesson is *how* to build; North Star is *what we're building toward*
