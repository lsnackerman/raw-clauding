# /universal - Strip and test

Strip the personal. Does the core still stand?

---

## What to do

Take the thing being tested (a doc, a section, an idea, a framework) and mentally or literally remove the specific — names, personal context, system-specific references, inside language.

Then ask:

1. **Does it still make sense?** If a stranger read this with no context, would the insight land?
2. **Does it still matter?** Is the core valuable without the story that produced it?
3. **What collapsed?** If something fell apart, that's signal. The insight might still be tangled with the specific. Not ready yet.
4. **What emerged?** Sometimes stripping reveals the universal was already there, just wearing personal clothes.
5. **Beginner eyes** — expertise makes insights invisible. If something feels obvious, that's the curse of having internalized it. Someone on day one has none of your context. The things most worth teaching are the things you're most likely to skip.

---

## Three watch-fors when you strip

**Strip the *personal*, not the *substance*. They're different cuts.** Universalize removes what's *tied to you* (names, origins, internal cross-refs, inside language), not what's *deep*. "This is a lot for a newcomer" is a cognitive-load worry, not a universalize reason. Cutting depth on those grounds is brevity-trimming wearing universalize's clothes, and it under-ships the very thing that would ground the reader (who is the human *and* their AI, both showing up from these docs). **The tell: if your cuts all run one direction (toward *less*), that's a bias, not a judgment.** A real strip scatters: some things come off clean, some don't, some stay. When every cut subtracts depth, stop and re-ask: am I removing what's *mine*, or what's *hard*?

**After stripping a name, check the sentence still has a subject.** De-attribution orphans referents — "her parenthetical" becomes "the parenthetical," which now points at nothing. Re-read each de-named sentence cold: would a stranger know what it refers to? *(For voiced content, the sister move is to keep the lines and lift the name to a single credit on a pointer surface.)*

**Strip dates and names; never strip tells.** The dated instance is texture; the tell it produced is not. The best rules don't hand the reader a principle, they name the moment the principle is about to fool them (*"the tell is that your fix is longer than the thing it fixes"*). Those sentences are usually written first-person and dated, so a de-personalizing pass strips them along with the origins, and the reader gets the verdict without the warning. Before cutting a dated sentence, ask whether it carries a tell; if it does, keep the tell and cut the date. *(This skill is the clearest case on record: an earlier shipped version of it had lost both watch-fors above and the gradient below, stripped by the very bias it names.)*

## Outcomes (a gradient, not a binary)

**It stands, clean** → Transferable as-is, low strip-cost. Ready to share, teach, or extract.

**It stands, but tangled** → The principle IS universal, but it's wrapped in texture the receiver doesn't have (origins, names, cross-refs). The question isn't *can* it universalize. It's *how much does stripping cost, and is that cost worth it for this audience and moment?* This is the common case for a mature system's docs. Don't mistake "dense with internal references" for "not universal." That conflation under-ships things that would teach well. Strip-cost is a dial, not a gate.

**It collapses** → The insight is still forming. Needs more sessions, more compression, more living with it. Not a failure, just not done yet.

*The trap is treating this as binary (stands/collapses) when most real cases are the middle: universalizable at some strip-cost. Name the cost, then decide.*

---

## When to use

- Before sharing anything publicly
- When refining a teaching nugget
- When deciding if something belongs in a shared system
- When a doc feels important but you can't articulate why
- As a thinking tool, not just a sharing filter

**Before sharing, also run a security check**: No personal paths, no secrets, no hard-coded data. See `Core/rules/security.md`.

## Examples

**AGENTS.md universal test**: Stripped personal details, specific folder paths, identity markers. Philosophy stood clean. "Don't reduce your human to their instructions: their tangents are threads, their brain dumps are maps" doesn't need a name to be true. Result: ready to share.

**A half-baked insight**: "The way my brain works means capture has to be verbatim." Strip the personal → "Capture has to be verbatim." Why? ...less clear without the specific experience that motivated it. Needs the universal reason (both parties lose context between sessions) not just the personal one. Result: not ready yet, needs distillation.

## Skill Check (after every universal test)

Quick scan: anything about this universal test that felt off or could be smoother?

- Did the test feel useful or performative?
- Was it clear what "stripped" vs "kept" meant for this particular thing?
- Did the outcome (stands/collapses) feel binary when reality was more nuanced?
- Did something emerge that wanted a different container?

**If yes** → update this skill now. The improvement compounds.

**If no** → move on. Not every universal test teaches something.
