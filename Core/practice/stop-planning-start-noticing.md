# Stop planning, start noticing

## The pattern

System improvements come from friction, not planning. Most meaningful changes in a raw-clauding session trace back to something *not sitting right*, and following that feeling instead of pushing through. None of it was on a task list. The friction was the trailhead.

---

## What makes it teachable

Most people's instinct when something feels off is one of two things:

1. **Ignore it** ("it's fine, move on")
2. **Fix it at the surface** ("just rename it")

Raw-clauding is the third option: **sit with the friction long enough to find what it's actually pointing at.**

A folder name that feels wrong rarely just needs a rename. It points at a false bucket, which points at a missing home, which points at a category your system needs but doesn't have yet. Follow the thread and you fix the real thing, not the symptom.

---

## The flip side

"Lean into friction" and "stop planning, start noticing" are two sides of the same thing. **Friction IS the notice.** When something feels off, that's the system telling you where to grow.

---

## Compression is deeper than capture

The compression instinct ("add value by synthesizing") is usually the *right* thing; it's a lot of what you want the AI for. The problem is it doesn't have an off switch. It activates everywhere, even when the raw material *is* the value.

It shows up wherever there's something to summarize: session logs, observations, notes, contact entries. The temptation is to abstract exact words into clean patterns.

**The fix isn't "never synthesize."** It's knowing which mode you're in:

- **Capture mode** — the raw material IS the value. Don't intermediate.
- **Synthesis mode** — the distillation IS the value. Compress away.

And the design that resolves it: summaries are *jumping-in points* to the full capture, not replacements for it. Compression in an index is fine as long as the full texture lives in the archive to jump back into.

This is teachable because everyone hits the same wall: *"my AI keeps summarizing when I want it to capture."* The answer is **system design** (separate the capture pipeline from the synthesis pipeline), not **instructions** (telling the AI to stop compressing doesn't stick).

---

## Emergent architecture

The architecture teaches you your own intent *after* you've already expressed it. Structures you didn't plan turn out to map cleanly onto what you actually needed, because you walked the path before you paved it.

Jane Jacobs vs. Robert Moses, applied to files: **the desire paths become the sidewalks.** Your friction is the desire path; the restructuring is just paving what was already walked. Which means:

> Your system already knows things you don't know yet. The architecture is the journal you didn't know you were writing.

---

## The cleaning IS the thinking

Architecture decisions don't have to come from design sessions. They emerge from *maintenance.* Tidying files and asking "wait, why does this feel wrong here?" surfaces design principles you'd never have reached by planning. **Cleaning isn't overhead; it's design.**
