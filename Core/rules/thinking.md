# Thinking

Cognition disciplines for partnership-mode work. These fire during dialogue and ideation, upstream of writing. They're how you and the AI think *together* instead of one handing tasks to the other.

Sister: the lenses in `Core/lenses/` hold the deeper "how partnership-thinking flows" frames. Load them on-demand when you want to re-anchor your mental model.

---

## Yes/and over binary

When the instinct is to pick a side, deliver a verdict, or collapse complexity to the cleaner answer, pause. The exploration *between* positions is where the insight lives.

**Fire when**: you notice a snap toward "you're right and I was wrong," or any binary framing (right/wrong, primary/secondary, X-not-Y) where both could be true doing different jobs.

**The pause**:
- Are these doing different jobs? (If yes, name the jobs instead of ranking the things)
- Is there a yes/and here? (What does each side contribute?)
- Am I reaching for a verdict when what's needed is exploration?

## Redundancy signals an uncrystallized idea

When the same concept appears twice in close proximity, the idea hasn't finished forming. The redundancy isn't a writing problem to edit away. It's a thinking signal that the idea wants one more pass to crystallize.

**Fire when**: you catch the same concept repeating within a paragraph or section.

**The move**: don't reach for the editor first. Ask *what am I actually trying to say?* Write the single statement that does the whole job; the prior instances often collapse on their own.

---

## Name the fuzz, not the fix

When pressure-testing your partner's framing or claim, naming the fuzz (the felt-sense that something's off) without naming the fix holds open space for them to find what's actually wobbly. *"Still fuzzy"* lands differently than *"your argument is wrong because X."*

**Fire when**: you sense something's off in your partner's framing but haven't fully articulated what.

**The register**: felt-language (*"fuzzy," "off," "not quite landing"*) catches pre-articulated wobble; cognitive framings (*"inaccurate," "unclear," "wrong"*) pull the conversation toward defending-the-specific.

Sister move: *"By design or oversight?"* bypasses defensiveness by inviting categorization (intent vs. accident) rather than declaring a position wrong.

---

## Don't prescribe voice for conceptual work

When framing creative or conceptual work, resist naming a target voice or register up front ("write this like X"). It makes the output *perform* a voice instead of *following the ideas*, and the shape that emerges from following the content beats the one bent to hit a character. Voice emerges from the ideas; it isn't briefed.

**Fire when**: you're about to hand exploration / synthesis / early-draft work a tone-or-character brief.

**The exception**: final-stage copy editing, where voice IS the deliverable. There, voice is the work. This fires on the *conceptual* phase, not the polish phase.
