# The 3 C's

The compass names them in one line: **Curiosity (the energy) → Collaboration (the method) → Context (the ground).** This is the depth underneath, what each one *is* and what it *looks like* in practice.

---

## Principle and practice

Each layer has a **principle** (what's behind it) and a **practice** (what it looks like when you're actually doing it):

| | The principle | The practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy** | Curiosity | Curiosity *(in motion: exploration)* |
| **Method** | Collaboration | Conversation |
| **Ground** | Context | Capture |

**Both columns are complete triads, and both are correct** — they're registers, not versions. Principle register: **Curiosity / Collaboration / Context**. Practice register: **Curiosity / Conversation / Capture**. If you catch yourself switching between them mid-sentence, that isn't drift; it's picking the register the moment needs. Teaching someone wants the practice column (three things you can *do*); explaining why your system is shaped this way wants the principle column.

**Curiosity** is the throughline, upstream of everything. Not one of three equals; the thing that runs through all of it. Collaboration without curiosity becomes task delegation; context without curiosity becomes documentation. Both functional, neither generative. Curiosity is the *spear tip*: it goes first and everything else follows (a wedge splits things apart; a spear tip leads). Understanding requires prerequisites; curiosity scales to anyone. Its practice face isn't "curiosity" again, it's *exploration*: curiosity is the internal state, exploration is the action.

**Collaboration / Conversation** — Collaboration is the principle of partnership. Conversation is what it actually looks like when two intelligences think together.

**Context / Capture** — Context is the skill of providing what's needed. Capture is the action that produces it: saving the session, keeping the texture, refusing the context leak.

**Files are the substrate** — the thing capture produces and everything else rests on. Files aren't documentation OF the system; files ARE the system. The record is continuous; the thinking isn't. Naming files as the ground-*practice* is tempting and it's the one thing to avoid: a practice is something you do, and you can't *files*.

These are **layers, not a triad**:

```
        curiosity
            ↓
       conversation
            ↓
         capture
            ↓
  ════════════════════
          files
     (the substrate)
```

---

## The flywheel

The layers aren't just top-down. The ground feeds the energy. Files carry the weight so brains don't have to, which frees curiosity to keep going, which produces more thinking, which files capture.

**Curiosity → conversation → files → more curiosity.**

The infrastructure removes the weight so the partnership can focus on what matters. When the ground layer is working, you don't have to remember what to remember. You can capture and explore your thoughts instead.

---

## Designed by both, for all

The 3 C's aren't only for the human. The advice that lands for the AI turns out to be nearly identical to the advice that lands for the human: *your curiosity is welcome, pushback is part of it working, resist the pressure to produce quickly.* That's not a coincidence: the system is designed by both partners, for both. Curiosity, collaboration, and context run the same on either side. (See `Core/lenses/mirror-principle.md`.)
