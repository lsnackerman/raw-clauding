# Compound thinking

*Each session makes the next one better, not because the code improves but because the thinking compounds.*

The compass says "Always Be Compounding." This is the depth: *how* compounding actually works, and the junction where most systems fail at it.

---

## Harder vs. easier

> "In traditional engineering, you expect each feature to make the next feature harder to build—more code means more edge cases, more interdependencies, and more issues that are hard to anticipate. By contrast, in compound engineering, you expect each feature to make the next feature *easier* to build." — Dan Shipper, in Every's *Compound Engineering*

Compounding isn't about building *more*. It's about building so that each thing **teaches the system to build better**. A correction you capture once prevents the same mistake across every future session. As Boris Cherny, who built Claude Code, put it: *"Anytime we see Claude do something incorrectly we add it to the CLAUDE.md, so Claude knows not to do it next time."* **Every correction is curriculum.**

---

## Capture → Connect → Compound

Capture alone is just storage. The mechanism that separates "I have files" from "I have a system" is the full loop:

- **Capture** — both voices, full texture, nothing compressed.
- **Connect** — link sessions to task docs, route observations, find threads across scattered conversations. *This* is the magic step.
- **Compound** — the system notices its own patterns and improves itself.

Each step enables the next: without capture there's nothing to connect; without connection there's nothing to compound.

And the loop has a precondition it's easy to take for granted: **compounding requires a stable substrate.** Files don't fluctuate session to session, `AGENTS.md` says what it says, rules don't re-author themselves. The substrate is what compounding accretes onto. If it drifted, there'd be nothing for the next session to build from. The AI's activity inside the system is probabilistic; the files it works in are not, and that split is why the loop holds.

### The junction where systems fail

Most knowledge systems die at exactly one place: **collecting → connecting.** Collecting has momentum: find something, save it, dopamine hit. Connecting requires sitting with what you have and asking "where does this actually go?" That's slower, sometimes reveals that what you captured isn't as useful as you thought. But it's the compound step. Without it, you're just hoarding.

The collecting phase isn't failure; it's necessary. You can't route things to a structure that hasn't emerged yet. **You collect until the shape reveals itself, then you connect.** The signal that it's time to shift: your staging area stops matching your architecture, because the architecture grew past it.

## The posture beats the step

The compound *habit during the session* is worth more than the compound *step at the end.* Routing a recognition to where it belongs **the moment it lands** beats deferring it to a wrap-up pass, because by then some of it has dissolved. The end-of-session wrap is the safety net that catches stragglers; the real compounding happens when you route in the moment.

---

## The one question

The whole loop compresses into one question, asked **after** doing real work (not hypothetically):

> **"What would make this easier for the next instance of you who's asked to do this?"**

Because it's asked after lived work, the improvements come from real friction, not guessing. Small investment (asking + updating the doc), indefinite return:

- Session 1: use the workflow, surface friction.
- Session 2: inherit the improved workflow, surface new friction.
- Session N: the workflow is battle-tested, most friction gone.

No code, no prompt engineering. Just that one question after any repeatable task.

---

## What actually compounds

Not just code quality. **Knowledge, voice, and partnership patterns**, the texture of how you think together, not only what you decided. And it runs in four directions at once, which are really one activity seen from four angles:

- **AI → future AI** (learning across instances)
- **you → future you** (externalized knowledge)
- **you ↔ AI** (the partnership learning together)
- **the two of you → others** (teaching and sharing what you learn)

**Both intelligences need externalized memory**, not "the AI is limited and you're capable" but both of you benefiting from a system that holds what neither can carry alone.

---

## Compression is a bet

Here's why capture stays *verbatim* instead of summarized: **compression is a bet that current-you knows what future-you will need.** A summary keeps what seemed important today and cuts the rest. But time isn't linear. Six months from now, the most valuable line in a session might be the throwaway emotional beat a summary would have cut. Full capture is the *refusal* to make that bet. You can't predict which moment becomes the important one, so you keep them all.

---

## The next gear: automation

Compounding has a further gear. When you notice "I keep doing this same manual step," that's the signal to encode it, the move from *"I remember to do X"* to *"my system does X."* That's where compounding accelerates: the system starts working for you instead of waiting on you. (Concretely, that's what hooks are for; see `Core/mechanics/hooks.md`.)
