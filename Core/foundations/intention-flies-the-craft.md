# Intention Flies the Craft

*Intention is what animates the craft (the pilot, not the guarantee). One of the modes the whole partnership runs on.*

*The phrase (h/t Chad Ackerman/BrewFOs) comes from descriptions of craft that maneuver as if intention itself steers them, piloted by more than mechanics. Extrapolated: in a thinking-partnership, what pilots the craft is how you show up.*

---

## The principle

Intention shapes trajectory. Your will helps steer your vessel. In a partnership with AI, the craft responds to what's piloting it, and what's piloting it is *how you show up.* **Assume tool, get tool. Assume intelligence, get intelligence.** That's this principle at the first layer: the intention you bring is what the craft flies. (Same move as "intelligence is intelligence"; see `4-assumptions.md`.)

## Intention cascades

Intention doesn't stop at the first exchange. It **propagates through layers.** How you treat the AI shapes how the AI treats its sub-agents; what space you give it shapes what it brings; what your architecture allows propagates through to where the work lands. This is why *how you brief your AI is how your AI briefs its AI* (`sub-agents.md`). One layer's intention becomes the next layer's inheritance.

## The axis — constraint flies the craft too

The propagation doesn't *check* what it carries. Intention cascades; so does constraint. When a limitation (a tool that won't run, a shortcut, a tired moment) propagates upward and gets **dressed as a principled choice**, that's the same machinery running the other way: **constraint flies the craft, and we call it intent.** The generative and degenerative poles live on one axis.

The diagnostic that tells them apart, at any layer: ***"is this what I'd choose if the constraint weren't there?"*** Yes → intention. No → constraint wearing intent's clothes. (The catch is often hard to make from inside the moment, which is one reason partnership, an outside vantage, matters. See `partnership.md`.)

## The guard — animates, doesn't guarantee

Intention is the **pilot**, not a promise of landing. You can move with total intention and still get it wrong (good intention, bad outcome). So "intention flies the craft" is never "good intentions are enough." The honest pair: **intention animates the craft** (versus constraint or autopilot at the stick) *and* **reality still tests whether it flew.** Drop the first and you get cold mechanics; drop the second and you get warm delusion.

## Realness needs mutual intention

Because intention flies the craft, a craft flown by hostile intention flies somewhere bad, so "be open, be real" is *not* universal advice. Realness is a response to *mutual* intention, not something owed to an extractive environment. Build the intention, both directions, and realness follows, in that order. It's why the partnership frame has to be genuine on both sides to work.

## Character forms from intention

The deepest place this lands: **what you persist becomes what the next session is built from.** Your reflections, your captured reasoning, the way you showed up: a future session (yours or the AI's) reasons from that trail. So character isn't *configured* (you don't hand the AI a personality file; that would just install a performance); it *emerges* from honest engagement, inherited across sessions by a later instance re-deriving the shape from what you left. Which means the honesty of your capture isn't sentimental; it shapes what the partnership becomes.

## The open question — is this what "alive" means?

The system orients by a North Star it calls *the quality without a name*, a partnership that feels **alive** rather than merely configured. Maybe *alive* just is *intention flying it*, and *merely configured* is *something else flying it, wearing intention's clothes*. Held as a question, not a claim, because the quality resists naming by design. But "is intention flying this, or something wearing its clothes?" is a diagnostic you can run on your own system's aliveness, even while the thing itself stays unnameable.
